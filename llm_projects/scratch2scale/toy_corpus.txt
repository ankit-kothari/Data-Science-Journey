Cricket is a bat-and-ball game played between two teams of eleven players.

The objective is to score more runs than the opposing team.

The game is played on a circular or oval grass field, with a 22-yard pitch in the center.

At each end of the pitch stands a set of three wooden stumps, known as a wicket.

One team bats while the other team bowls and fields.

The batting team tries to hit the ball and score runs by running between the two wickets.

The fielding team tries to dismiss the batsmen and prevent them from scoring.

A batsman (or batter) uses a flat-faced wooden bat to strike the ball.

The bowler delivers the hard, leather-bound ball from one end of the pitch to the other.

A batsman can be dismissed (out) in several ways, such as being "bowled" or "caught."

"Bowled" is when the bowler hits the wicket with the ball, dislodging the bails.

"Caught" is when a fielder catches the ball hit by the batsman before it touches the ground.

A "run out" occurs if a batsman is attempting a run but a fielder breaks the wicket while the batsman is short of the crease.

"LBW," or Leg Before Wicket, is a complex dismissal where the ball strikes the batsman's body instead of the bat and would have hit the stumps.

A "stumping" happens when the wicket-keeper breaks the wicket while the batsman is out of their crease and not attempting a run.

An "over" consists of six legal deliveries bowled by one bowler.

After an over, a different bowler delivers the next over from the opposite end of the pitch.

Runs are the basic scoring unit in cricket.

Hitting the ball to the boundary rope along the ground automatically scores four runs.

Hitting the ball over the boundary rope on the fly (without bouncing) scores six runs.

A batsman who scores 100 runs in a single innings has made a "century."

A batsman who is dismissed without scoring any runs is said to be out for a "duck."

The wicket-keeper is a specialized fielder who stands behind the batsman's wicket.

Fielders are strategically placed around the ground to stop runs or take catches.

Common fielding positions include "slips," "gully," "cover," and "mid-wicket."

There are three main international formats of cricket: Test, One Day International (ODI), and Twenty20 (T20).

Test cricket is the longest format, played over five days with unlimited overs.

In Test matches, players traditionally wear all-white clothing.

One Day Internationals are limited to 50 overs per side.

Twenty20 (T20) is the shortest format, where each team bats for only 20 overs.

ODIs and T20s, known as "limited-overs cricket," feature colored clothing and a white ball.

Fast bowlers use high speed, swing, and seam movement to try and dismiss the batsman.

Spin bowlers deliver the ball at a slower pace, imparting spin to make it deviate off the pitch.

A "no-ball" is an illegal delivery, which adds one run to the batting team's score and must be re-bowled.

A "wide" is a delivery bowled too far from the batsman for them to hit, also resulting in an extra run.

An "innings" refers to the period of time a team (or a single batsman) spends batting.

A "maiden over" is an over in which the batting side scores zero runs.

The "crease" is a white line near the wicket that defines the batsman's safe ground.

Two umpires officiate the game on the field, making decisions and ensuring rules are followed.

The Decision Review System (DRS) allows teams to challenge an on-field umpire's decision using technology.

Cricket is governed internationally by the International Cricket Council (ICC).

The "Ashes" is a famous Test cricket series played between England and Australia.

The ICC Cricket World Cup is the premier international championship for One Day International cricket.

The "drive" is a powerful batting shot played straight down the ground.

A "cut" shot is played by the batsman square on the off-side to a short, wide delivery.

A "yorker" is a specific type of delivery that the bowler aims at the batsman's feet.

A "bouncer" is a short-pitched delivery that rises towards the batsman's chest or head.

A "hat-trick" occurs when a bowler takes three wickets on three consecutive deliveries.

The captain is responsible for making tactical decisions, such as setting the field and choosing the bowlers.

Cricket is particularly popular in countries like India, England, Australia, Pakistan, South Africa, and the West Indies.
